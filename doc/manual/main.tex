\chapter{\texttt{Dwm::What::Info}}
\texttt{Dwm::What::Info} is just a \texttt{Dwm::What::SegmentedLiteral}
with a specific delimiter (not user-specified), an automatically added
first segment and named accessors for the contained segments.

\section{Why a specific 2-byte delimiter between segments?}
\subsection{Desirable delimiter features}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Painless to eliminate from the vocabulary allowed in
the user-specified content.  We want something that the user
will never need.
\item Does not clutter the display of the entire literal
(including the delimiters) such as that from \texttt{data\_view()}
and \texttt{view()}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Human readability of strings in binary files.
\item Machine readability of strings in binary files (the
ability to decompose into segments), without compromising the
human readability.
\end{enumerate}

I've chosen Unicode {\em No-Break Space (NBSP)} as the delimiter,
since it serves the needs well.  For a human it just looks like
a single space, while it can be distinguished from a single space
when parsing.

\section{Equivalence to \texttt{Dwm::What::SegmentedLiteral}}
The following will produce the equivalent stirng literal.  This makes
it obvious that \texttt{Dwm::What::Info} is just a
\texttt{Dwm::What::SegmentedLiteral} with a specific delimiter
(\texttt{"\textbackslash{xC2}\textbackslash{xA0}"}) and an automatically added first segment
(\texttt{"@(\#)"}).

\begin{verbatim}
   SegmentedLiteral("\xC2\xA0", "@(#)",
                    DWM_WHAT_TYPE_LIB, DWM_WHAT_STATUS_REL, "DwmWhat",
                    "1.0.0", "Daniel McRobb 2025", "mcplex.net");

               Info(DWM_WHAT_TYPE_LIB, DWM_WHAT_STATUS_REL, "DwmWhat",
                    "1.0.0", "Daniel McRobb 2025", "mcplex.net");
\end{verbatim}
